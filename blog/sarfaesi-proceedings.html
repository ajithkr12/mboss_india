Understanding SARFAESI Proceedings in India

Banking Law MBOSS Lexon LLP November 20, 2024 10 min read
🏦
MBOSS LEXON LLP · BANKING LAW

What is the SARFAESI Act?

The Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 (SARFAESI Act) is one of India's most significant banking laws. It empowers banks, financial institutions and non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) to enforce their security interests without court intervention when a borrower defaults.

Before SARFAESI, lenders had to go to civil courts for recovery of secured debts — a process that could take years. SARFAESI dramatically changed this by allowing lenders to take direct enforcement action against defaulting borrowers and their secured assets.

Who Can Use SARFAESI?

SARFAESI can be invoked by:

  • Scheduled Commercial Banks (nationalised and private)
  • State Financial Corporations
  • National Housing Bank
  • NBFCs with asset size above ₹100 crore (as per RBI criteria)
  • Asset Reconstruction Companies (ARCs)

When Can SARFAESI Be Invoked?

SARFAESI can be invoked when:

  • The borrower's account has been classified as a Non-Performing Asset (NPA) per RBI guidelines
  • The outstanding debt is ₹1 lakh or more
  • The security interest is over immovable or movable property
  • The debt is secured by a valid security interest

Important: SARFAESI does not apply to agricultural land, loans below ₹1 lakh, or unsecured debts.

SARFAESI Enforcement Procedure — Step by Step

Step 1: NPA Classification

The borrower's account must be classified as NPA per RBI's Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning (IRACP) norms — typically when the account remains overdue for 90 days or more.

Step 2: Section 13(2) Demand Notice

The lender issues a written demand notice to the borrower (and guarantors) requiring repayment of the entire outstanding dues within 60 days. The notice must specifically mention the amount due, the secured assets, and the lender's intention to enforce security if payment is not made.

Step 3: Borrower's Right to Representation

Within the 60-day period, the borrower can make a written representation or objection to the lender. The lender must consider the representation and communicate the reasons for acceptance or rejection within 15 days.

Step 4: Possession Notice (Section 13(4))

If the borrower fails to repay within 60 days, the lender can take any or all of the following enforcement measures:

  • Take possession of the secured assets (physical or constructive)
  • Take over management of the business of the borrower
  • Appoint a manager to manage the secured assets
  • Require a debtor of the borrower to pay directly to the secured creditor

Step 5: Sale of Secured Assets

The secured creditor can sell the secured assets (immovable property by e-auction; movable property by public auction or private sale) after giving 30 days' notice to the borrower. The sale proceeds are applied toward the outstanding debt, with any surplus returned to the borrower.

Borrower Rights Under SARFAESI

SARFAESI provides certain protections to borrowers:

  • Right to challenge under Section 17 — Borrower can approach the Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT) within 45 days of possession notice to challenge the enforcement action
  • Right to redeem — Before sale completion, borrower can repay entire dues and redeem the secured asset
  • Right to representation — Borrower can make representation against Section 13(2) notice
  • Surplus payment — Borrower is entitled to receive any sale surplus after full debt recovery

Common SARFAESI Challenges by Borrowers

Borrowers often challenge SARFAESI proceedings on grounds including:

  • Improper NPA classification
  • Procedural defects in notice (wrong particulars, inadequate time)
  • Debt genuinely not due (payment disputes)
  • Security interest not properly perfected
  • Account not covered under SARFAESI (agricultural land, etc.)

SARFAESI vs. DRT vs. Civil Court — Which to Choose?

Lenders have multiple recovery options. SARFAESI is the fastest for secured debt recovery. DRT proceedings (Original Application under Recovery of Debts Act) can run parallel to SARFAESI. Civil court suits are generally the slowest and are typically used for unsecured debts or complex disputes.

MBOSS Lexon LLP evaluates each recovery situation and recommends the optimal strategy — often combining SARFAESI enforcement with DRT proceedings for maximum effectiveness.

FAQ — SARFAESI Proceedings
Can a borrower stop SARFAESI enforcement?+

A borrower can challenge SARFAESI proceedings before the DRT under Section 17 within 45 days of the enforcement action. The DRT can grant a stay on enforcement if the borrower demonstrates a strong prima facie case. However, the DRT generally requires a deposit of a portion of the dues before granting a stay. MBOSS Lexon LLP advises borrowers on Section 17 challenges.

What is the timeline for SARFAESI enforcement?+

Typical SARFAESI timeline: Section 13(2) notice (60 days) → Borrower representation (15 days for lender response) → Possession action → 30-day sale notice → Auction/sale. If unchallenged, enforcement from notice to sale can be completed in 4-6 months. Challenges at DRT can extend this significantly.

Does SARFAESI apply to NBFCs?+

Yes. RBI has extended SARFAESI applicability to NBFCs with asset size of ₹100 crore or more. The RBI periodically updates the list of NBFCs eligible to use SARFAESI. MBOSS Lexon LLP advises NBFCs on their SARFAESI eligibility and enforcement procedures.
