Startup Legal Checklist Before Raising Investment

Why Legal Preparation Matters Before Fundraising

Raising investment is a pivotal moment for any startup. But poorly prepared startups often face delays, reduced valuations, or deal collapses because of legal issues discovered during investor due diligence. Investors — whether angels, VCs or family offices — conduct thorough legal due diligence before investing. Any gaps can derail or significantly delay a funding round.

This checklist covers the essential legal steps Kerala and Indian startups must complete before approaching investors for seed funding, angel investment, or Series A rounds.

1. Corporate Structure — Get it Right

The foundation of any investable startup is a properly structured legal entity.

  • Incorporate as a Private Limited Company — LLPs cannot issue equity shares to investors. Convert to a Pvt Ltd before fundraising.
  • Verify MCA compliance — Ensure all annual filings (Form MGT-7, AOC-4) are up to date with no penalties pending.
  • Clean cap table — Maintain an accurate, updated capitalisation table showing all shareholders and their holdings.
  • Authorised share capital — Ensure your authorised capital is sufficient to accommodate the proposed investment without immediate alteration.

2. Founder Agreements — Protect the Core

Investor due diligence always examines founder relationships. Unresolved founder issues are a major red flag.

  • Founder Shareholder Agreement — Clearly define each founder's roles, responsibilities, decision-making authority and share ownership.
  • Vesting Schedule — Implement a standard 4-year vesting schedule (with 1-year cliff) for all founder shares. This protects investors from founder exits.
  • IP Assignment Agreements — Every founder must sign an IP assignment agreement transferring all startup-related intellectual property to the company.
  • Non-compete and Non-solicitation — Founders should have appropriate restrictions on competitive activities.

3. Intellectual Property — Secure Your Innovations

IP protection is critical before disclosure to investors or the market.

  • Trademark Registration — File trademark applications for your brand name, logo and product names before public launch or investor discussions.
  • Copyright — Ensure all software code, content and creative work is owned by the company through proper assignment agreements.
  • Confidentiality NDAs — Have all employees, contractors and advisors sign NDAs before sharing proprietary information.
  • Domain Registration — Secure all relevant domain names.

4. Employment and Contractor Documentation

Investor due diligence examines employment practices closely.

  • Employee Agreements — All employees must have signed employment agreements including IP assignment and confidentiality clauses.
  • Contractor Agreements — Freelancers and contractors must have service agreements with clear IP ownership provisions.
  • Offer Letters and Appointment Letters — Properly documented for all team members.
  • ESOP — If you plan to offer ESOPs, have a board-approved ESOP plan and grant letters in place.

5. Regulatory and Compliance Status

Any compliance gaps discovered during due diligence can delay or block investment.

  • GST Registration and Returns — Current and up to date
  • Professional Tax and TDS — All deductions filed and paid
  • DPIIT Recognition — Obtain DPIIT startup recognition before fundraising to benefit from Angel Tax exemption
  • Sector-specific Licences — Verify all required licences and registrations are in place

6. DPIIT Recognition — Critical for Angel Tax

Angel Tax (Section 56(2)(viib) of Income Tax Act) can apply when shares are issued to investors at a valuation premium. DPIIT-recognised startups are exempt from Angel Tax — making recognition essential before raising angel investment. MBOSS Lexon LLP assists with DPIIT recognition applications.

7. Investment Documentation — Be Prepared

Have these documents ready or prepared for investor discussions:

  • Term Sheet — Understand and be ready to negotiate standard VC/angel term sheet provisions including valuation, anti-dilution, liquidation preference, and board rights.
  • Shareholders Agreement (SHA) — Know what investor rights provisions (information rights, tag-along, drag-along, right of first refusal) investors typically require.
  • Data Room — Organise a virtual data room with all legal, financial and corporate documents for due diligence.

8. Data Room Checklist

Your data room should include:

  • Certificate of Incorporation and MCA documents
  • MOA, AOA and all amendments
  • Shareholders register and cap table
  • All board and shareholder resolutions
  • Audited financial statements
  • All material contracts and agreements
  • IP registrations and assignments
  • Employment agreements
  • DPIIT recognition certificate
  • Regulatory licences and compliance certificates
FAQ — Startup Fundraising Legal
What is Angel Tax and how can I avoid it?+

Angel Tax applies under Section 56(2)(viib) of the Income Tax Act when a startup raises funds at a valuation above fair market value. The excess is treated as income. DPIIT-recognised startups with investor income tax compliance are exempt. MBOSS Lexon LLP advises on DPIIT recognition to achieve Angel Tax exemption before fundraising.

How much does legal preparation for fundraising cost?+

Legal preparation costs depend on what is already in place. For a startup with basic compliance, preparation for a seed round (DPIIT, ESOP, agreements, data room) typically costs ₹30,000 to ₹1,00,000 depending on complexity. MBOSS Lexon LLP provides transparent, fixed-scope pricing for startup fundraising preparation.

What is a typical timeline for legal preparation before fundraising?+

Allow 4-8 weeks for full legal preparation including DPIIT recognition, document drafting, IP filings and compliance cleanup. DPIIT recognition alone takes 4-6 weeks. Starting legal preparation before investor conversations begin is strongly advisable.
